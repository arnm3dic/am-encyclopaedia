## Summary

White is the clearest example of a symbol split by geography rather than by
period: a wedding colour across much of the modern West and a funeral colour
across much of East Asia, with both readings current at the same time.

## Core meanings

- **Purity and innocence** — from the absence of mark or stain; the metaphor is literal before it is moral.
- **Mourning** — the colour of bone and of the undyed, plain cloth worn when display is set aside.
- **Surrender and truce** — a blank flag carries no allegiance, which is the point.
- **Candidacy and office** — Roman office-seekers wore whitened togas; *candidatus* gives English "candidate".

## By tradition

### Roman

*Candidus* is bright or whitened white, distinct from *albus*, plain white. The
whitened toga of a man standing for office made the claim visible, and the word
for the practice outlasted the practice itself in most European languages.

### Chinese and Japanese

White is the mourning colour, worn at funerals and associated with death ritual;
the same association makes white an awkward choice for gifts and celebration.
In the five-element scheme white corresponds to metal, to the west, and to
autumn — the declining part of the cycle.

### Western (modern)

The white wedding dress is recent and traceable: Victoria's 1840 marriage made
it fashionable, and the purity reading was attached to it afterwards rather than
being its cause. Before that, brides married in their best dress of whatever
colour.

## Inversions and tensions

The wedding/funeral split means white is one of the few symbols where a
well-intentioned choice reliably reads as an insult across cultures, and it is
worth noting that neither reading is older or more authentic — they derive from
different features of the same colour. White's association with purity also
carries a racial history in modern Europe and America that an entry on the
colour should name rather than treat as unrelated.
